## Adverbs in -ment

Most are formed from the **feminine** adjective + **-ment** (= English *-ly*):

| adjective (m → f) | adverb |
|---|---|
| lent → lente | [[lentement]] (slowly) |
| heureux → heureuse | [[heureusement]] (fortunately) |
| doux → douce | [[doucement]] (gently, quietly) |
| facile (ends in -e) | [[facilement]] |

- Adjectives ending in a **vowel** use the masculine: vrai → [[vraiment]], poli → [[poliment]], absolu → [[absolument]].
- **-ant → -amment**, **-ent → -emment** (both pronounced *-amã*): courant → [[couramment]] (fluently), évident → [[évidemment]] (obviously), récent → [[récemment]].
- Irregular: gentil → [[gentiment]], bref → [[brièvement]], précis → [[précisément]].

## Bon / bien, mauvais / mal

**bon** (adjective) describes a noun; **bien** (adverb) describes a verb:
[[C'est un bon livre.]] / [[Il parle bien.]] · [[Ce gâteau est bon.]] (tastes good) / [[Ça sent bon.]] (smells good — fixed)
**mauvais** / **mal**: [[un mauvais film]] / [[J'ai mal dormi.]]
**vite** is an adverb (*fast*); **rapide** is the adjective: [[une voiture rapide]] / [[Il conduit vite.]]

## Position

- After the conjugated verb: [[Il parle souvent de toi.]] [[Je mange rarement de la viande.]]
- In compound tenses, **short** adverbs go between auxiliary and participle: [[J'ai bien dormi.]] [[Il a déjà mangé.]] [[Tu as beaucoup travaillé.]] Long *-ment* adverbs usually go after: [[Il a parlé lentement.]]
- Time and place adverbs go at the start or end: [[Hier, il a plu.]] [[Je t'attends ici.]]
