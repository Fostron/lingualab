## Masculine or feminine?

Every French noun is **masculine** or **feminine**, and you usually can't guess it from the meaning. **Always learn nouns with their article**: not *maison* but [[la maison]].

## The definite article — "the"

| | before a consonant | before a vowel or mute h |
|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | **le** — [[le livre]] | **l'** — [[l'homme]], [[l'arbre]] |
| feminine singular | **la** — [[la maison]] | **l'** — [[l'eau]], [[l'école]] |
| plural (both genders) | **les** — [[les livres]], [[les maisons]] | **les** + liaison — [[les amis]] |

Because *l'* hides the gender, learn vowel-initial nouns with **un / une**: [[un arbre]], [[une école]].

## French uses "the" much more than English

1. For things in general: [[J'aime le café.]] — I like coffee. [[Les enfants aiment les bonbons.]]
2. With languages, countries, abstract ideas: [[Le français est beau.]] [[la France]] [[la liberté]]
3. With days for a habit: [[le lundi]] — on Mondays.
4. With body parts: [[J'ai mal à la tête.]] — My head hurts.

## Some clues to gender (with many exceptions)

| usually masculine | usually feminine |
|---|---|
| -age: [[le fromage]] (but *la plage, la page, l'image*) | -tion, -sion: [[la question]] |
| -ment: [[le moment]] | -té: [[la liberté]] |
| -eau: [[le bateau]] (but *l'eau, la peau*) | -ette, -elle, -esse: [[la fourchette]] |
| -isme: [[le tourisme]] | -ure, -ance, -ence: [[la voiture]], [[la chance]] |
| days, months, seasons, languages | most nouns ending in -e (not always!) |

> Watch out for words whose gender differs from what you'd expect: [[la personne]] (always feminine, even for a man), [[le problème]], [[le système]].

## Contractions with à and de

| | à (to, at) | de (of, from) |
|---|---|---|
| + le | **au**: [[au cinéma]] | **du**: [[le livre du professeur]] |
| + la | à la: [[à la plage]] | de la: [[la porte de la maison]] |
| + l' | à l': [[à l'école]] | de l': [[le nom de l'hôtel]] |
| + les | **aux**: [[aux États-Unis]] | **des**: [[la fin des vacances]] |
