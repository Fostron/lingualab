## The partitive article: some (of a whole)

For things you **can't count** — an unspecified amount of a substance or abstract thing — French uses the **partitive article**:

| | |
|---|---|
| masculine | **du** — [[du pain]], [[du café]] |
| feminine | **de la** — [[de la viande]], [[de la musique]] |
| before a vowel | **de l'** — [[de l'eau]], [[de l'argent]] |
| plural | **des** — [[des pâtes]], [[des légumes]] |

- [[Je mange du pain.]] — I'm eating (some) bread.
- [[Tu veux de l'eau ?]] — Do you want (some) water?
- [[Il a de la chance.]] — He's lucky.
- [[Elle fait du sport.]] — She does sport.

English often uses no article; French needs one.

## Partitive or definite?

After verbs of **liking** (*aimer, adorer, préférer, détester*), use **le / la / les** — you're talking about the thing in general:
[[J'aime le fromage.]] (I like cheese — in general) but [[Je mange du fromage.]] (I'm eating some cheese).

## After a negation → de

[[Je bois du café.]] → [[Je ne bois pas de café.]] · [[Il y a de l'eau.]] → [[Il n'y a pas d'eau.]]

## Quantities → de

After expressions of quantity, only **de / d'** (no article):
| | |
|---|---|
| [[beaucoup de]] | a lot of — [[beaucoup de travail]] |
| [[un peu de]] | a little — [[un peu de sucre]] |
| [[trop de]] / [[assez de]] | too much / enough |
| [[combien de]] | how much / many |
| [[un kilo de]], [[un litre de]], [[une bouteille de]] | [[une bouteille de vin]] |
| [[un morceau de]], [[une tranche de]] | a piece, a slice |

But [[la plupart des gens]] (most people) and [[bien des fois]] keep *des*.
