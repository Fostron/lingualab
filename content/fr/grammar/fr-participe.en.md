## The present participle (-ant)

Form: *nous* stem + **-ant** (*parlant, finissant, faisant*; irregular *étant, ayant, sachant*). It is **invariable**.

It is mainly written and replaces a relative clause with **qui** or a cause:
- [[Les personnes désirant participer doivent s'inscrire.]] = *qui désirent* — People wishing to take part must register.
- [[Ne sachant pas quoi dire, il est parti.]] = *comme il ne savait pas* — Not knowing what to say, he left.
- [[Étant malade, elle n'est pas venue.]] — Being ill, she didn't come.

**Compound form** (*ayant / étant* + participle) for an earlier action: [[Ayant fini son travail, il est rentré.]] — Having finished his work… · [[Étant arrivés en retard, nous avons raté le début.]]

## Participle vs verbal adjective

Some *-ant* forms are also **adjectives** — then they agree and describe a quality. Some even change spelling:
| participle (action, invariable) | adjective (quality, agrees) |
|---|---|
| [[des gens parlant fort]] | [[des films parlants]] |
| [[en fatiguant]] | [[une journée fatigante]] |
| [[en convainquant]] | [[des arguments convaincants]] |
| [[négligeant]] | [[négligent]] |
| [[précédant]] | [[précédent]] |
| [[différant]] | [[différent]] |

## Absolute participle clauses (formal)

The participle can have its own subject: [[Le temps passant, il a oublié.]] — As time passed… · [[La nuit tombée, ils sont rentrés.]] — Once night had fallen… · [[Le travail terminé, nous sommes sortis.]] — With the work done, we went out.

## Past participle clauses

[[Arrivée à Paris, elle a appelé sa mère.]] — Having arrived in Paris… (the participle agrees with the subject) · [[Une fois la décision prise, on ne recule plus.]]
