## Direct object pronouns

They replace a noun that receives the action directly (*I see **the house*** → *I see **it***):

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **me / m'** — me | **nous** — us |
| 2nd | **te / t'** — you | **vous** — you |
| 3rd | **le / l'** — him, it (m) · **la / l'** — her, it (f) | **les** — them |

They go **before the verb** — the opposite of English:
- [[Tu vois Paul ? — Oui, je le vois.]]
- [[Tu aimes cette chanson ? — Oui, je l'aime.]]
- [[Il prend les clés ? — Oui, il les prend.]]
- [[Je t'aime.]] — I love you. [[Tu m'entends ?]] — Can you hear me?

## Position in different structures

| structure | position | example |
|---|---|---|
| simple tense | before the verb | [[Je le connais.]] |
| negation | *ne* + pronoun + verb + *pas* | [[Je ne le connais pas.]] |
| verb + infinitive | before the **infinitive** | [[Je vais le faire.]] [[Tu peux m'aider ?]] |
| passé composé | before the **auxiliary** | [[Je l'ai vu.]] [[Je ne l'ai pas vu.]] |
| affirmative imperative | after, with a hyphen | [[Prends-le !]] [[Regarde-moi.]] |

## Agreement in the passé composé

When a direct object pronoun comes **before** *avoir*, the past participle agrees with it:
[[Ces photos ? Je les ai prises hier.]] [[La lettre ? Je l'ai écrite.]] (You'll study this in more depth at B2.)

## Le for ideas

**le** can replace a whole idea: [[Je le sais.]] — I know (it). [[Je le pense aussi.]] — I think so too.
