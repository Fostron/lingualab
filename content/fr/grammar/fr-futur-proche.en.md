## Futur proche: aller + infinitive

**aller (present) + infinitive** = *going to*. It's the most common way to talk about the future in everyday French.

- [[Je vais manger.]] — I'm going to eat.
- [[Qu'est-ce que tu vas faire ce week-end ?]]
- [[Il va pleuvoir.]] — It's going to rain.
- [[Nous allons visiter Rome.]]
- [[Ils vont arriver demain.]]

Negative: *ne … pas* around **aller**: [[Je ne vais pas sortir ce soir.]]
Pronouns go before the infinitive: [[Je vais le faire.]] [[Tu vas te lever ?]]

## Passé récent: venir de + infinitive

**venir (present) + de + infinitive** = to have **just** done:
- [[Je viens d'arriver.]] — I've just arrived.
- [[Elle vient de partir.]] — She has just left.
- [[Nous venons de manger.]]

## Être en train de + infinitive

To stress that something is happening **right now**: [[Je suis en train de travailler.]] — I'm (busy) working.

## Summary

| | |
|---|---|
| [[Je viens de manger.]] | I've just eaten (recent past) |
| [[Je suis en train de manger.]] | I'm eating right now (in progress) |
| [[Je vais manger.]] | I'm going to eat (near future) |

Time words: [[maintenant]] · [[tout de suite]] (right away) · [[bientôt]] (soon) · [[tout à l'heure]] (later today / a little while ago) · [[ce soir]] · [[demain]] · [[déjà]] (already)
