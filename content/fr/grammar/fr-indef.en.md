## The indefinite article — "a, an, some"

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | **un** — [[un livre]] | **des** — [[des livres]] |
| feminine | **une** — [[une maison]] | **des** — [[des maisons]] |

English often uses no article in the plural (*I have books*), but French needs **des**: [[J'ai des livres.]] — I have (some) books.

## Un/une/des → de in negative sentences

After a negation, *un, une, des* (and the partitive *du, de la*) become **de / d'**:
- [[J'ai un chien.]] → [[Je n'ai pas de chien.]]
- [[Il y a des enfants.]] → [[Il n'y a pas d'enfants.]]

Except after *être*: [[Ce n'est pas un livre, c'est un cahier.]]

## Un/une or le/la?

**un / une** introduces something new or non-specific; **le / la** refers to something specific or known:
[[Il y a un café dans la rue. Le café est très bon.]]

No article before a profession or nationality after *être*: [[Il est médecin.]] — He's **a** doctor.

## Plural of nouns

| rule | example |
|---|---|
| add **-s** (silent) | [[une maison]] → [[des maisons]] |
| **-s, -x, -z** don't change | [[un fils]] → [[des fils]], [[un prix]] → [[des prix]] |
| **-eau, -eu** → **-x** | [[un bateau]] → [[des bateaux]], [[un jeu]] → [[des jeux]] |
| **-al** → **-aux** | [[un journal]] → [[des journaux]], [[un animal]] → [[des animaux]] |
| irregular | [[un œil]] → [[des yeux]], [[madame]] → [[mesdames]], [[monsieur]] → [[messieurs]] |

Since the *-s* is silent, you hear the plural through the article: *le livre / les livres* — *leuh / lé*.
