French is spoken by over 300 million people on five continents. Standard French (the one in this course) is understood everywhere, but regional varieties differ in vocabulary, pronunciation and sometimes grammar.

## Québec (Canada)

Strong pronunciation differences (*tu* → "tsu", *dire* → "dzire") and many specific words, often resisting English loanwords that France accepts:
| France | Québec | English |
|---|---|---|
| [[le week-end]] | [[la fin de semaine]] | weekend |
| [[faire du shopping]] | [[magasiner]] | shopping |
| [[le petit-déjeuner]] / [[le déjeuner]] / [[le dîner]] | [[le déjeuner]] / [[le dîner]] / [[le souper]] | breakfast / lunch / dinner |
| [[un e-mail]] | [[un courriel]] | email |
| [[une voiture]] | [[un char]] | car |
| [[c'est cool]] | [[c'est le fun]] / [[c'est écœurant]] | it's great |
| [[ma copine]] | [[ma blonde]] | my girlfriend |
| [[se garer]] | [[se stationner]] | to park |

Also *Bonjour !* used to say goodbye, and the question particle *-tu*: [[Tu viens-tu ?]] (Are you coming?).

## Belgium and Switzerland

[[le déjeuner, le dîner, le souper]] for breakfast, lunch, dinner (as in Québec) · Belgium: [[une drache]] (heavy rain), [[s'il vous plaît]] when handing something (= *voilà*), [[savoir]] for *pouvoir* (*je ne sais pas venir* = I can't come) · Switzerland: [[un natel]] (mobile phone), [[une panosse]] (mop), [[septante]].

## Africa

French is an official or major language in over 20 African countries; Kinshasa and Abidjan are among the largest French-speaking cities in the world. Local French has its own words: [[un maquis]] (open-air restaurant, Ivory Coast), [[une essencerie]] (petrol station, Senegal), [[un deuxième bureau]] (a mistress), [[enceinter]] (to make pregnant).

## Français standard

For writing and formal speech, standard French is the norm everywhere. Recognise the varieties, and choose one accent to model your pronunciation on.
