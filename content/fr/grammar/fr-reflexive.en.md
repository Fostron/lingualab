## Pronominal verbs

Many French verbs are used with a **reflexive pronoun** (*se* in the infinitive): [[se lever]] (to get up), [[s'appeler]] (to be called). The pronoun matches the subject:

| | se lever |
|---|---|
| je **me** lève | nous **nous** levons |
| tu **te** lèves | vous **vous** levez |
| il / elle / on **se** lève | ils / elles **se** lèvent |

*me, te, se* → **m', t', s'** before a vowel: [[Je m'appelle Léa.]] [[Il s'habille.]]

- [[Je me lève à sept heures.]]
- [[Tu te couches tard ?]]
- [[Nous nous promenons dans le parc.]]
- [[Ils s'amusent bien.]]

## Daily routine

[[se réveiller]] (wake up) · [[se lever]] (get up) · [[se doucher]] (shower) · [[se laver]] (wash) · [[se brosser les dents]] (brush one's teeth) · [[s'habiller]] (get dressed) · [[se dépêcher]] (hurry) · [[se reposer]] (rest) · [[se promener]] (go for a walk) · [[s'asseoir]] (sit down) · [[se coucher]] (go to bed)

With body parts French uses **le / la / les**, not *my*: [[Je me lave les mains.]] — I wash my hands.

## Negation and infinitives

- Negative: *ne* before the pronoun: [[Je ne me lève pas tôt.]]
- With an infinitive, the pronoun still matches the subject: [[Je vais me coucher.]] [[Tu dois te reposer.]]
- Imperative: [[Lève-toi !]] [[Asseyez-vous.]] but [[Ne te lève pas !]]

## Not always "oneself"

Many pronominal verbs simply have a different meaning: [[s'appeler]] (be called), [[se souvenir de]] (remember), [[s'occuper de]] (take care of), [[se passer]] (happen), [[se tromper]] (be wrong), [[s'en aller]] (leave). Plural pronominal verbs can be reciprocal: [[Ils s'aiment.]] — They love each other.
