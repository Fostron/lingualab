French spelling looks complicated, but it is far more regular than English. Learn the patterns below and you'll be able to read aloud almost anything. Click the examples to hear them.

## Silent letters

- **Final consonants are usually silent**: [[petit]], [[vous]], [[grand]], [[les]]. Exceptions: words ending in **c, r, f, l** often pronounce them (think *CaReFuL*): [[avec]], [[bonjour]], [[neuf]], [[il]] — but *-er* verbs are silent: [[parler]].
- **Final -e** is silent: [[table]], [[elle]]. It makes the consonant before it audible: [[petit]] vs [[petite]].
- **h** is never pronounced: [[homme]], [[hôtel]].
- The verb ending **-ent** is silent: [[ils parlent]] sounds like *il parle*.

## Vowels

| spelling | sound | example |
|---|---|---|
| **ou** | *oo* in *food* | [[vous]], [[rouge]] |
| **u** | *ee* with rounded lips (no English equivalent) | [[tu]], [[rue]] |
| **eau, au, o** | *o* | [[beau]], [[chaud]] |
| **ai, è, ê** | *e* in *bed* | [[mais]], [[père]], [[fête]] |
| **é, -er, -ez** | closed *ay* (short, no glide) | [[été]], [[parler]], [[assez]] |
| **oi** | *wa* | [[moi]], [[voiture]] |
| **eu, œu** | like *ur* in *fur* without the r | [[deux]], [[sœur]] |

> Tell **tu** (you) from **tout** (all) and **vu** (seen) from **vous** (you): it's the most important vowel contrast in French.

## Nasal vowels

A vowel + **n** or **m** at the end of a syllable becomes nasal (air through the nose, the *n* isn't pronounced):
| | |
|---|---|
| **an, en, am, em** | [[enfant]], [[temps]] |
| **on, om** | [[bon]], [[nom]] |
| **in, im, ain, ein, un** | [[vin]], [[pain]], [[un]] |

Before a vowel or double *n/m*, the vowel is not nasal: [[bonne]], [[année]].

## Consonants

**r** is pronounced at the back of the throat: [[rue]], [[Paris]]. **ch** = *sh*: [[chat]]. **gn** = *ny*: [[montagne]]. **j** and **g** before e/i = *zh* (as in *measure*): [[je]], [[rouge]]. **ç** = *s*: [[français]]. **qu** = *k*: [[qui]]. **ill** after a vowel = *y*: [[fille]], [[travailler]].

## Accents

**é** (closed e), **è / ê** (open e), **à, ù** (distinguish words: *a/à, ou/où*), **ç** (soft c), **ë / ï** (pronounce vowels separately: *Noël*). Accents are part of spelling — missing ones count as mistakes.

## Liaison and elision

- **Liaison**: a normally silent final consonant is pronounced before a vowel: [[vous avez]] → *vou-za-vé*, [[les amis]] → *lé-za-mi*, [[un enfant]].
- **Elision**: *le, la, je, me, te, se, de, ne, que* drop their vowel before a vowel or mute h: [[l'homme]], [[j'aime]], [[c'est]], [[il n'est pas]].

## Stress

French has no word stress: syllables are even, and the **last syllable of a phrase** is slightly longer.
