## My, your, his/her…

The possessive agrees with the **thing owned**, not with the owner:

| owner | masc. sing. | fem. sing. | plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| je | **mon** | **ma** | **mes** |
| tu | **ton** | **ta** | **tes** |
| il / elle | **son** | **sa** | **ses** |
| nous | **notre** | **notre** | **nos** |
| vous | **votre** | **votre** | **vos** |
| ils / elles | **leur** | **leur** | **leurs** |

- [[mon père]], [[ma mère]], [[mes parents]]
- [[ton livre]], [[ta voiture]], [[tes amis]]
- [[notre maison]], [[nos enfants]] · [[votre adresse]], [[vos papiers]] · [[leur fils]], [[leurs enfants]]

## Son / sa / ses = his OR her

French doesn't distinguish the owner's gender — only the noun's:
[[son frère]] = his brother **or** her brother; [[sa voiture]] = his car **or** her car.
To make it clear: [[son frère à elle]] (her brother).

## Mon, ton, son before a vowel

Before a feminine noun starting with a vowel or mute h, use **mon, ton, son** (for the sound): [[mon amie]] (my female friend), [[ton école]], [[son histoire]].

## Possession with de

There's no *'s*: [[le livre de Pierre]] — Pierre's book. [[la maison de mes parents]] — my parents' house.
