## "A / an" and "some"

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| masculine | **un** libro — a book | **unos** libros — some books |
| feminine | **una** casa — a house | **unas** casas — some houses |

- [[Tengo un perro y una gata.]] — I have a dog and a (female) cat.
- [[Hay unos libros en la mesa.]] — There are some books on the table.
- *unos / unas* before a number means "about": [[unos veinte euros]] — about twenty euros.

Like *el*, **un** is used before feminine nouns with a stressed *a-*: [[un águila]], [[un hacha]].

## When Spanish leaves the article out

1. **Professions, nationality, religion after *ser*** (unless there is an adjective):
   [[Soy profesor.]] — I am **a** teacher. But: [[Es un profesor muy bueno.]]
2. After **tener** / **buscar** / **llevar** when the number doesn't matter, especially in questions and negatives:
   [[¿Tienes coche?]] — Do you have a car? [[No tengo hermanos.]]
3. Before **otro** (another), **cien**, **mil**, **medio**: [[otro café]] = another coffee (never *un otro*), [[mil euros]].

## When to use *el* vs *un*

*un/una* introduces something new or non-specific; *el/la* refers to something known or unique:
[[Hay un restaurante en la calle. El restaurante es muy bueno.]]
