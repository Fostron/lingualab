## Verbs like gustar

Several common verbs work like *gustar*: the person is an indirect object (*me, te, le…*) and the verb agrees with the **thing**.

| verb | meaning | example |
|---|---|---|
| [[doler]] (o→ue) | to hurt | [[Me duele la cabeza.]] — I have a headache. |
| [[encantar]] | to love (things) | [[Me encantan los museos.]] |
| [[interesar]] | to interest | [[¿Te interesa la política?]] |
| [[importar]] | to matter, to mind | [[No me importa.]] — I don't mind. |
| [[molestar]] | to bother | [[Me molesta el ruido.]] |
| [[parecer]] | to seem | [[¿Qué te parece?]] — What do you think? |
| [[faltar]] | to be missing, lack | [[Me falta tiempo.]] |
| [[quedar]] | to have left; to suit | [[Nos quedan dos días.]] [[Te queda bien.]] |
| [[apetecer]] (Spain) | to feel like | [[¿Te apetece un café?]] |

- [[Me duelen los pies.]] — My feet hurt. (plural → *duelen*)
- [[A mi madre le duele la espalda.]]
- [[Me parece una buena idea.]] — I think it's a good idea.

## Health

[[¿Qué te pasa?]] — What's wrong? [[¿Cómo te encuentras?]] — How are you feeling?

| | |
|---|---|
| [[Tengo fiebre.]] | I have a fever. |
| [[Tengo dolor de cabeza / estómago / garganta.]] | I have a headache / stomachache / sore throat. |
| [[Estoy resfriado.]] | I have a cold. |
| [[Tengo gripe.]] | I have the flu. |
| [[Me siento mal.]] | I feel ill. |
| [[Tengo tos.]] | I have a cough. |

> Spanish uses **the**, not *my*, with body parts when the owner is clear: [[Me duele la mano.]] — My hand hurts.
