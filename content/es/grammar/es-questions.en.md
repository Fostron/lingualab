## Question words

All question words carry a **written accent** — both in direct and indirect questions.

| | |
|---|---|
| [[¿Qué?]] | What? Which? |
| [[¿Quién?]] / [[¿Quiénes?]] | Who? (sing. / pl.) |
| [[¿Dónde?]] | Where? — [[¿Adónde?]] Where to? — [[¿De dónde?]] Where from? |
| [[¿Cuándo?]] | When? |
| [[¿Cómo?]] | How? (also: Pardon?) |
| [[¿Cuánto?]] / [[¿Cuánta?]] / [[¿Cuántos?]] / [[¿Cuántas?]] | How much? / How many? |
| [[¿Cuál?]] / [[¿Cuáles?]] | Which (one)? What? |
| [[¿Por qué?]] | Why? — answer: [[porque]] = because |

- [[¿Qué haces?]] — What are you doing?
- [[¿Quién es ella?]] — Who is she?
- [[¿Cómo te llamas?]] — What's your name?
- [[¿Por qué estudias español? — Porque me gusta.]]

**Qué vs cuál** before *ser*: use **cuál** to ask for one item from a set, **qué** to ask for a definition.
[[¿Cuál es tu número de teléfono?]] (What's your phone number?) vs [[¿Qué es un móvil?]] (What is a "móvil"?)

**Prepositions go first**: [[¿De dónde eres?]] (Where are you from?), [[¿Con quién vives?]] (Who do you live with?), [[¿Para qué?]] (What for?).

## Word order in questions

The verb usually comes right after the question word, and the subject (if any) after the verb:
[[¿Dónde vive tu hermano?]] — Where does your brother live?

## Negation

- **no** goes before the verb: [[No trabajo los domingos.]]
- Double negatives are normal: **no** + verb + *nada / nadie / nunca*:
  [[No veo nada.]] — I don't see anything. [[No hay nadie.]] — There's nobody. [[No como nunca carne.]] = [[Nunca como carne.]]
- **también** (also) ↔ **tampoco** (neither): [[Yo también.]] / [[Yo tampoco.]]
