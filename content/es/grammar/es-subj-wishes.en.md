## Influence: wanting, asking, advising

When one person wants **someone else** to do something, the second verb goes into the **subjunctive**:

**[subject 1] + verb of influence + que + [subject 2] + subjunctive**

- [[Quiero que vengas.]] — I want you to come.
- [[Mi madre prefiere que no salgamos.]] — My mother prefers us not to go out.
- [[Te pido que me ayudes.]] — I'm asking you to help me.
- [[El médico me recomienda que haga deporte.]]
- [[Espero que estés bien.]] — I hope you're well.
- [[Necesito que me llames.]]

Verbs of this type: *querer, desear, preferir, esperar, necesitar, pedir, rogar, exigir, aconsejar, recomendar, sugerir, permitir, prohibir, dejar, mandar, insistir en, decir* (when it means *to tell someone to do*).

## Same subject → infinitive

If there's **no change of subject**, use the infinitive, not *que*:
| same subject | different subject |
|---|---|
| [[Quiero ir.]] — I want to go. | [[Quiero que vayas.]] — I want you to go. |
| [[Espero aprobar.]] — I hope to pass. | [[Espero que apruebes.]] — I hope you pass. |

## Decir: report or order?

[[Me dice que viene.]] — He tells me he's coming (information → indicative).
[[Me dice que venga.]] — He tells me to come (order → subjunctive).

## Ojalá

**ojalá (que)** + subjunctive = *I hope, if only* (from Arabic *"if God wills"*). It doesn't need a main verb:
[[¡Ojalá haga sol mañana!]] [[Ojalá que tengas suerte.]]

## Good wishes

Spanish wishes often start with **que** + subjunctive (an implied *I hope*):
[[¡Que te diviertas!]] — Have fun! [[¡Que tengas un buen día!]] [[¡Que aproveche!]] — Enjoy your meal! [[¡Que te mejores!]] — Get well soon! [[¡Que descanses!]]
