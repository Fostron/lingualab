## Direct object pronouns

A direct object receives the action directly: *I see **the house*** → *I see **it***.

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **me** — me | **nos** — us |
| 2nd | **te** — you | **os** — you all |
| 3rd masc. | **lo** — him, it, you (Ud.) | **los** — them, you (Uds.) |
| 3rd fem. | **la** — her, it, you (Ud.) | **las** — them, you (Uds.) |

The pronoun replaces the noun and agrees with it in **gender and number**:
- [[¿Tienes el libro? — Sí, lo tengo.]]
- [[¿Ves la casa? — Sí, la veo.]]
- [[¿Compras los zapatos? — No, no los compro.]]
- [[¿Conoces a mis hermanas? — Sí, las conozco.]]
- [[Te quiero.]] — I love you. [[¿Me ayudas?]] — Will you help me?

## Position

1. **Before a conjugated verb** (and before *no*): [[Lo veo.]] [[No lo veo.]]
2. **Attached to an infinitive or gerund**, or placed before the first verb — both are correct:
   [[Voy a comprarlo.]] = [[Lo voy a comprar.]]
   [[Estoy leyéndola.]] = [[La estoy leyendo.]] (add an accent to keep the stress)
3. **Attached to an affirmative command**: [[¡Cómelo!]] — Eat it! (but [[¡No lo comas!]])

## The personal a

When the direct object is a **specific person** (or a pet), put **a** in front of it:
[[Veo a María.]] [[Busco a mi hijo.]] [[Quiero a mi perro.]]
But not with things or unspecified people: [[Veo la casa.]] [[Busco un médico.]] And not after *tener*: [[Tengo dos hermanos.]]

## Lo as "it" for ideas

**lo** can stand for a whole idea: [[No lo sé.]] — I don't know (it). [[Lo siento.]] — I'm sorry. [[Ya lo veo.]] — I can see that.

> In parts of Spain you will hear *le* instead of *lo* for a male person (*le veo* = I see him). This is called *leísmo* and is accepted for people, but *lo* is always correct.
