The little word **se** has several jobs. You already know two: reflexive (*se levanta*) and *se* replacing *le/les* before *lo/la* (*se lo doy*). Here are three more.

## 1. Impersonal se — "people, one, you"

**se + 3rd person singular verb**, with no specific subject:
- [[Aquí se vive bien.]] — People live well here / Life is good here.
- [[¿Cómo se dice "apple" en español?]] — How do you say…?
- [[No se puede fumar.]] — Smoking isn't allowed.
- [[Se come muy bien en este restaurante.]]

## 2. Passive se — "is / are done"

**se + verb agreeing with the thing**: singular thing → singular verb, plural → plural.
- [[Se vende piso.]] — Flat for sale (lit. a flat is sold).
- [[Se venden pisos.]] — Flats for sale.
- [[Se habla español.]] — Spanish spoken.
- [[Aquí se reparan bicicletas.]] — Bikes repaired here.
- [[Se necesitan camareros.]] — Waiters needed.

This is the most natural way to express the passive in Spanish (much more common than *ser + participle*).

## 3. Accidental se — "it happened to me"

For unplanned events, Spanish says the thing **did it by itself to you**:
**se + indirect pronoun (me, te, le…) + verb (agreeing with the thing)**

| | |
|---|---|
| [[Se me olvidó.]] | I forgot (it slipped my mind). |
| [[Se me olvidaron las llaves.]] | I forgot the keys. |
| [[Se le cayó el vaso.]] | He dropped the glass. |
| [[Se nos rompió la tele.]] | Our TV broke. |
| [[Se me perdió el móvil.]] | I lost my phone. |
| [[Se te acabó el tiempo.]] | Your time is up. |
| [[Se me ocurre una idea.]] | An idea occurs to me. |

This takes the blame off the person — compare [[Rompí el vaso.]] (I broke it, maybe on purpose) with [[Se me rompió el vaso.]] (it broke on me).
