## Subject pronouns

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | [[yo]] — I | [[nosotros]] / [[nosotras]] — we |
| 2nd (informal) | [[tú]] — you | [[vosotros]] / [[vosotras]] — you all (Spain) |
| 2nd (formal) | [[usted]] — you | [[ustedes]] — you all |
| 3rd | [[él]] — he, [[ella]] — she | [[ellos]] / [[ellas]] — they |

- **tú** is for friends, family, children, people your age. **usted** (often written *Ud.*) is polite: strangers, older people, formal situations. Grammatically, *usted* takes the **he/she** form of the verb.
- In Latin America **ustedes** is the only plural "you" (both friendly and formal). In Spain, **vosotros** is the friendly plural.
- The **-as** forms (*nosotras, vosotras, ellas*) are for groups of only women; any mixed group uses the masculine.
- Spanish usually **drops** subject pronouns because the verb ending already shows the person: [[Soy estudiante.]] = I am a student. Use the pronoun for emphasis or contrast: [[Yo soy de Madrid, y él es de Lima.]]

## Ser — "to be" (identity)

| | |
|---|---|
| yo **soy** | nosotros **somos** |
| tú **eres** | vosotros **sois** |
| él / ella / usted **es** | ellos / ellas / ustedes **son** |

Use **ser** for what something *is* — things that define it:

- **identity, name**: [[Soy Ana.]] [[Es mi hermano.]]
- **profession**: [[Ella es médica.]] (no *una* before a profession!)
- **origin, nationality** (with *de*): [[Somos de México.]] [[¿De dónde eres?]]
- **characteristics**: [[El libro es interesante.]]
- **time and dates**: [[Son las tres.]] [[Hoy es lunes.]]
- **material**: [[La mesa es de madera.]]

> Spanish has a second verb "to be", **estar**, for location and temporary states. You will meet it in a few units.

## Negation and questions

Put **no** before the verb: [[No soy profesor.]] For a yes/no question, just use question marks and rising intonation: [[¿Eres estudiante?]] — [[Sí, soy estudiante.]] / [[No, no soy estudiante.]]
