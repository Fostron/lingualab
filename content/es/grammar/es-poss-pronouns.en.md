## Mine, yours, his…

Long (stressed) possessives agree with the **thing owned**:

| owner | masc. sing. | fem. sing. | masc. pl. | fem. pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| yo | **mío** | **mía** | **míos** | **mías** |
| tú | **tuyo** | **tuya** | **tuyos** | **tuyas** |
| él / ella / Ud. | **suyo** | **suya** | **suyos** | **suyas** |
| nosotros | **nuestro** | **nuestra** | **nuestros** | **nuestras** |
| vosotros | **vuestro** | **vuestra** | **vuestros** | **vuestras** |
| ellos / Uds. | **suyo** | **suya** | **suyos** | **suyas** |

## Three ways to use them

1. **After ser** (no article): [[Este libro es mío.]] — This book is mine. [[¿Es tuya esta maleta?]]
2. **As pronouns with an article** (to replace a noun): [[Tu coche es rojo; el mío es azul.]] — Your car is red; mine is blue. [[Mis padres viven en Madrid. ¿Y los tuyos?]]
3. **After a noun** (*of mine*): [[un amigo mío]] — a friend of mine. [[¡Dios mío!]] — My God!

**suyo** is ambiguous (his, hers, theirs, yours) — clarify with *de*: [[el de ella]], [[el de ustedes]].

## Lo mío / lo tuyo

The neuter **lo** + possessive = *my thing / what's mine*: [[Lo mío es la música.]] — Music is my thing.
