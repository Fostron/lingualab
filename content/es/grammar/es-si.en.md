## Three kinds of if-sentences

| type | *si* clause | main clause | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **real / likely** | present indicative | present, future or command | [[Si llueve, me quedo en casa.]] [[Si tienes tiempo, llámame.]] [[Si estudias, aprobarás.]] |
| **hypothetical (present / future)** | imperfect subjunctive | conditional | [[Si tuviera dinero, viajaría.]] — If I had money, I would travel. |
| **impossible (past)** | pluperfect subjunctive | conditional perfect | [[Si hubiera estudiado, habría aprobado.]] — If I had studied, I would have passed. (B2) |

## Rules to remember

1. **Never** use the present subjunctive after *si* (if): ~~*si tenga*~~.
2. **Never** use the future or the conditional in the *si* clause: ~~*si tendré*, *si tendría*~~ → **si tengo / si tuviera**.
3. The clauses can swap places: [[Viajaría más si tuviera dinero.]]

## Hypotheses

- [[Si fuera rico, compraría una casa en la playa.]]
- [[¿Qué harías si ganaras la lotería?]]
- [[Si yo fuera tú, hablaría con ella.]] — If I were you, I'd talk to her.
- [[Si no estuviera tan cansado, saldría.]] — (but I am tired)
- [[Si vivieras más cerca, nos veríamos más.]]

## Si = whether

In indirect questions *si* means *whether* and takes the indicative (even the future or conditional): [[No sé si vendrá.]] [[Me preguntó si podría ayudarle.]]

## Other ways to say "if"

**de + infinitive**: [[De haberlo sabido, no habría venido.]] — Had I known… · **en caso de que** + subj. — in case · **con tal de que** + subj. — provided that.
