## Que — the all-purpose relative

**que** (no accent) means *who, which, that* and works for people and things. Unlike English, it can **never be left out**.

- [[El libro que leo es interesante.]] — The book (that) I'm reading is interesting.
- [[La chica que vive aquí es mi prima.]] — The girl who lives here…
- [[Las fotos que me enviaste son preciosas.]]

## Quien / quienes — for people

Used for people **after a preposition** (*with whom, to whom…*) or in non-essential clauses between commas:
- [[La persona con quien hablé era muy amable.]] — The person I talked to…
- [[Mi hermano, quien vive en Lima, viene mañana.]]
- At the start of a sentence = *whoever*: [[Quien busca, encuentra.]]

## Donde — where

[[La ciudad donde nací es pequeña.]] — The city where I was born… [[el restaurante donde comimos]]

## Lo que — what (the thing that)

[[Lo que necesito es dormir.]] — What I need is to sleep. [[No entiendo lo que dices.]] — I don't understand what you're saying. [[Haz lo que quieras.]] — Do what you want.

Compare with the question word **qué**: [[¿Qué dices?]] (What are you saying?) vs [[No sé qué dices.]] (indirect question: accent!) — but *lo que* has no accent.

## Prepositions come first

English can leave a preposition at the end (*the man I spoke **to***), Spanish cannot:
[[el hombre con quien hablé]], [[la casa en la que vivo]], [[el amigo de quien te hablé]].

After a preposition, *que* usually needs an article: **el que, la que, los que, las que** (you'll study these more at B1).
