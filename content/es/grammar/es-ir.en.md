## Ir — to go

| | |
|---|---|
| yo **voy** | nosotros **vamos** |
| tú **vas** | vosotros **vais** |
| él / ella / usted **va** | ellos / ellas / ustedes **van** |

**ir** is completely irregular, but its present looks like an *-ar* verb with the stem *v-*.

## Ir a + place

Destination takes **a**, and **a + el = al**:
- [[Voy a Madrid.]] — I'm going to Madrid.
- [[Vamos al cine.]] — We're going to the cinema.
- [[¿Vas a la playa?]]
- [[¿Adónde vas?]] — Where are you going?

Note: **ir a casa** — to go home (no article). **ir de compras / de vacaciones / de viaje** — to go shopping / on holiday / on a trip.

Transport uses **en**: [[en tren]], [[en autobús]], [[en coche]], [[en avión]] — but **a pie** (on foot).

## Ir a + infinitive: the near future

**ir a + infinitive** = *going to*. It is the most common way to talk about plans in everyday Spanish.
- [[Voy a estudiar esta noche.]] — I'm going to study tonight.
- [[¿Qué vas a hacer mañana?]] — What are you going to do tomorrow?
- [[Va a llover.]] — It's going to rain.
- [[No vamos a salir.]]

**¡Vamos!** = Let's go! **Vamos a + infinitive** can also mean *let's…*: [[¡Vamos a comer!]]

## Al and del

| | |
|---|---|
| a + el = **al** | [[Voy al parque.]] |
| de + el = **del** | [[Vengo del trabajo.]] |
| a la, de la, a los, de las… | no contraction |

> Only the article **el** contracts, never the pronoun **él** (he): [[Es el coche de él.]] — It's his car.
