## Indirect object pronouns

An indirect object is the person **to whom / for whom** something is done: *I give **him** the book*.

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **me** — (to) me | **nos** — (to) us |
| 2nd | **te** — (to) you | **os** — (to) you all |
| 3rd | **le** — (to) him, her, you (Ud.) | **les** — (to) them, you (Uds.) |

Verbs of giving and communicating take indirect objects: *dar, regalar, mandar, enviar, prestar, decir, contar, explicar, enseñar, preguntar, escribir*.

- [[Le doy el libro.]] — I give him/her the book.
- [[¿Me prestas tu bolígrafo?]] — Can you lend me your pen?
- [[Les escribo una carta.]]
- [[Te explico la regla.]]

Spanish often keeps the pronoun **even when the person is named** — this is normal, not redundant:
[[Le doy el regalo a mi madre.]] [[Les digo la verdad a mis padres.]]

Position: the same as direct object pronouns (before the conjugated verb, attached to infinitive / gerund / affirmative command).

## Two pronouns together

When both appear, the **indirect comes first**: [[Me lo das.]] — You give it to me. [[Te la explico.]] — I'll explain it to you.

**le / les + lo / la / los / las → se lo / se la / se los / se las**
(*le lo* doesn't exist in Spanish.)

| | |
|---|---|
| [[Le doy el libro.]] | → [[Se lo doy.]] — I give it to him. |
| [[Les mando las fotos.]] | → [[Se las mando.]] — I send them to them. |
| [[Voy a dárselo.]] | I'm going to give it to him/her. (note the accent) |

Because *se* can mean him, her, them or you, add *a + person* if needed: [[Se lo doy a ella.]]
