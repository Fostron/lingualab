## Three verb families

Spanish infinitives end in **-ar**, **-er** or **-ir**. To conjugate a regular verb, remove the ending to get the **stem** and add the personal endings. The ending tells you who is doing the action, so the pronoun is usually dropped.

## -ar verbs: hablar (to speak)

| | ending | |
|---|---|---|
| yo | **-o** | [[hablo]] |
| tú | **-as** | [[hablas]] |
| él / ella / usted | **-a** | [[habla]] |
| nosotros | **-amos** | [[hablamos]] |
| vosotros | **-áis** | [[habláis]] |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **-an** | [[hablan]] |

Most Spanish verbs are **-ar** verbs and most of them are regular: *trabajar, estudiar, comprar, llamar, tomar, llegar, mirar, escuchar, esperar, necesitar, ayudar, cocinar, bailar, cantar, pagar, viajar*.

- [[Hablo inglés y un poco de español.]]
- [[¿Trabajas aquí?]]
- [[Mi padre cocina muy bien.]]
- [[Estudiamos español los lunes.]]
- [[¿Qué buscáis?]]
- [[Ellos llegan mañana.]]

## What the present tense covers

The Spanish present is used for:

1. Habits and facts: [[Trabajo en una oficina.]] — I work in an office.
2. Actions happening now: [[¿Qué miras?]] — What are you looking at?
3. The near future (with a time word): [[Mañana compro el pan.]] — I'll buy the bread tomorrow.
4. Offers / requests: [[¿Te ayudo?]] — Shall I help you?

> English *do / does* doesn't exist here: [[¿Hablas español?]] = Do you speak Spanish? [[No hablo alemán.]] = I don't speak German.

## Spelling notes

- **llegar → llego, pagar → pago** (regular in the present; spelling changes only appear later in the past tense).
- The stress falls on the stem in *hablo, hablas, habla, hablan* and on the ending in *hablamos, habláis*.
