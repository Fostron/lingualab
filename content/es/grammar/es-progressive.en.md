## Estar + gerund

To stress that something is happening **right now**, use **estar + gerund** (the *-ing* form):

| verb type | ending | example |
|---|---|---|
| -ar | **-ando** | hablar → [[hablando]] |
| -er / -ir | **-iendo** | comer → [[comiendo]], vivir → [[viviendo]] |

- [[Estoy hablando por teléfono.]] — I'm talking on the phone.
- [[¿Qué estás haciendo?]] — What are you doing?
- [[Están viendo una película.]]
- [[Está lloviendo.]] — It's raining.

## Irregular gerunds

| | |
|---|---|
| vowel + -er/-ir → **-yendo** | leer → [[leyendo]], oír → [[oyendo]], traer → [[trayendo]], ir → [[yendo]] |
| -ir stem change e → i | pedir → [[pidiendo]], decir → [[diciendo]], seguir → [[siguiendo]], vestir → [[vistiendo]] |
| -ir stem change o → u | dormir → [[durmiendo]], morir → [[muriendo]] |
| poder | [[pudiendo]] |

## Present vs present progressive

Spanish uses the progressive **less** than English. The simple present often covers "now":
[[¿Qué haces?]] = What are you doing? — perfectly natural.

Use *estar + gerund* to emphasize that the action is in progress or temporary:
[[Estoy trabajando en casa esta semana.]] — I'm working from home this week.

Never use it for the future (English *I'm leaving tomorrow* → [[Me voy mañana.]]) and avoid it with *ir, venir, tener, estar*.

## Pronouns

Object and reflexive pronouns go before *estar* or attach to the gerund (add an accent):
[[Me estoy duchando.]] = [[Estoy duchándome.]]

## Other gerund patterns

- **seguir + gerund** = to keep doing / still: [[Sigo estudiando español.]]
- **llevar + time + gerund** = to have been doing: [[Llevo dos años viviendo aquí.]] (more at B2)
